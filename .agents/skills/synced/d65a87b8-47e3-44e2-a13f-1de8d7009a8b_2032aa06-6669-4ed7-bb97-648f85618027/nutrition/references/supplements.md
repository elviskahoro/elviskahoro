# Supplement Decision Framework
## For a Natural Bodybuilder in Body Recomposition

---

## Guiding Principle

Supplements fill gaps. They do not replace whole food. They do not accelerate fat loss on their own. The hierarchy is: training consistency → sleep → whole food nutrition → hydration → supplements.

That said, for a natural bodybuilder in a cut, a few supplements are well-supported by evidence and practically useful.

---

## Tier 1: Evidence-Strong, Widely Used

### Creatine Monohydrate
**What it does:** Increases phosphocreatine stores in muscle, improving high-intensity performance. Supports muscle volume and strength output, especially during a caloric deficit.

**Why it matters during a cut:** Muscle is harder to maintain in a deficit. Creatine supports performance so training stays productive.

**Dose:** 3–5g daily. Timing doesn't matter much — consistency matters. Take it every day, not just training days.

**Scale note:** Creatine causes 1–3 lbs of water retention in muscle tissue. This is a positive adaptation (fuller muscles), not fat. Weigh this when interpreting scale during initial use.

**Form:** Creatine monohydrate — the most researched, least expensive, most effective form. Fancy branded versions are not meaningfully better.

---

### Protein Powder
**What it does:** Convenient dietary protein. Not magic — it's food in powder form.

**When to use:** When whole food protein is inconvenient or when hitting daily targets requires a top-up.

**What to look for:**
- Whey isolate (faster digesting, lower lactose, lower fat/carbs) for post-workout
- Casein (slower digesting) for before bed if appetite is low at night
- For dairy sensitivity: egg white protein powder

**What to avoid:**
- Protein powders with added sugar, proprietary blends, or more than 5g carbs per scoop on a cut
- "Meal replacement" shakes that are calorie-dense — check the label

**Dose:** 1 scoop = 20–25g protein. No more than 2 scoops per day — get the rest from whole food.

---

### Electrolytes
**What they do:** Sodium, potassium, magnesium — regulate fluid balance, muscle contraction, nerve function. Critical during a cut (especially low-carb), because low carbs = lower glycogen = lower water retention = higher electrolyte excretion.

**Signs of electrolyte deficit:** Muscle cramps, headaches, fatigue, poor performance, brain fog.

**During a cut:** Electrolytes matter more than when eating at maintenance. Don't skip them.

**Practical options:**
- LMNT packets (high sodium, no sugar — good for low-carb) — dissolve in water
- Nuun tablets (lower sodium, good during training)
- Lite salt (potassium + sodium, cheap, add a pinch to water)
- Pickle juice — unprocessed, surprisingly effective for cramps

---

## Tier 2: Situationally Useful

### Caffeine
**What it does:** Improves performance, focus, and fat oxidation. Well-researched for athletic use.

**For a cut:** Useful as a pre-workout and appetite suppressant. Black coffee counts.

**Dose:** 150–300mg pre-workout (varies by tolerance). Do not exceed 400mg/day. Do not use within 6 hours of sleep.

**Note:** Tolerance builds quickly. Cycling off every 4–6 weeks maintains effectiveness.

---

### Magnesium (Glycinate or Malate form)
**What it does:** Supports sleep quality, muscle relaxation, and reduces cortisol. Highly underrated during a cut.

**Why:** Stress + caloric deficit + training volume = elevated cortisol. Cortisol impairs fat loss and disrupts sleep. Magnesium mitigates this.

**Dose:** 200–400mg magnesium glycinate before bed.

**Note:** Magnesium oxide (the cheap kind at drugstores) has poor absorption. Glycinate or malate are worth the slightly higher cost.

---

### Vitamin D3 + K2
**What it does:** D3 supports muscle function, immune health, mood, and hormonal health. K2 ensures D3 is used correctly (routes calcium to bones, not arteries).

**Most people in the US — especially in San Francisco with fog and indoor work — are deficient.**

**Dose:** 2,000–5,000 IU D3 with K2 (100–200mcg) daily, taken with a fat-containing meal.

---

## Tier 3: Skip or Low Priority for Natural Bodybuilding

**Fat burners / thermogenics:** Usually just caffeine + stimulants in a proprietary blend at an inflated price. No evidence of significant added fat loss beyond what caffeine alone provides.

**BCAAs:** If you're hitting your daily protein target from whole food and protein powder, BCAAs are redundant. Spend the money on food.

**Collagen protein:** Not a complete protein (missing tryptophan). Does not count toward your daily protein target in the way whey or egg does. Useful for joint health supplementally — not as a protein source.

**Pre-workout with excessive stimulants:** If it has more than 300mg caffeine per scoop, assess whether you need that load consistently. Adrenal fatigue is real, and it impairs fat loss and recovery.

---

## Stack Summary (Cut Phase)

| Supplement | Dose | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Creatine monohydrate | 5g | Any time, daily |
| Whey isolate (if needed to hit protein) | 1 scoop | Post-workout or as needed |
| Electrolytes | 1 packet/tablet | During or after training, or as needed |
| Caffeine / black coffee | 150–300mg | Pre-workout, not within 6 hrs of sleep |
| Magnesium glycinate | 300mg | Before bed |
| Vitamin D3 + K2 | 2,000–5,000 IU + K2 | With a meal containing fat |
