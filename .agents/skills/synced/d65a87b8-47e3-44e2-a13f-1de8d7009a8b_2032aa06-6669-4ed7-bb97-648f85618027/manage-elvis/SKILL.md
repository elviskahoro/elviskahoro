# Manage Elvis: Essential Life Skills Teaching Framework
## Aligned to M's 7-Pillar Life OS

The curriculum maps to M's Life OS 3.0 seven-pillar system. Each pillar has staged skill progression from basics to competency. This ensures Elvis's growth is integrated into the same operating system M runs her entire life on.

## The Seven Pillars

| Pillar | Color | Focus for Elvis |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| 1. Health | Red | Personal hygiene, fitness, nutrition, sleep |
| 2. Systems | Orange | Household management, routines, time management, adulting essentials |
| 3. Network | Yellow | Social skills, communication, friendships, community |
| 4. Core (Self) | Green | Emotional regulation, self-awareness, integrity, personal growth |
| 5. Dreams | Light Blue | Goals, aspirations, planning for the future, vision |
| 6. Vocation | Indigo | Career skills, professional development, work ethic |
| 7. Wealth | Purple | Personal finances (basics, middle, advanced) |

## The Learner Profile

Elvis is an adult learner with the following characteristics:

**How he learns best:**
- Physical demonstration (watching someone do it, then doing it together)
- Written sequential steps he can reference independently
- Clear start point ("here's where we are now") and clear end goal ("here's what done looks like")
- Every step shown explicitly, nothing assumed
- Stages and phases, not all at once
- Multiple pathways to the same goal (options, not mandates)
- Gentle correction without shame
- Repetition and practice, not lecture

**What doesn't work:**
- Verbal instructions alone (he won't retain them)
- Assumed knowledge ("you should know this already")
- Abstract goals without concrete steps
- Criticism without instruction
- Being told WHAT to do without being shown HOW

**Learning challenges:**
- Possible ADHD patterns (difficulty with follow-through, task switching, executive function)
- Freeze response under emotional pressure (shuts down, says "I'm tired," avoids)
- Tends to substitute easier tasks for harder committed ones (the Taco Bell Promise pattern)
- Responds better to systems than to willpower
- Needs external structure until internal structure develops

**Reference materials available:**
- ADHD Home Cleaning Checklist book
- Kids Basic Hygiene Checklist book
- Adulting 101 book (grocery shopping, basic life management)
- Dave Ramsey Baby Steps curriculum (M teaches this class)

## The Teaching Method: SEEDS

Every lesson follows the SEEDS framework:

**S** - State where we're starting (current skill level, no judgment)
**E** - End goal (clear picture of what competency looks like)
**E** - Every step shown (smallest possible sequential steps, nothing assumed)
**D** - Demonstrate or document (physical demo or written reference)
**S** - Stage it (one skill per week, build on previous, celebrate progress)

## Pillar 1: HEALTH (Red)

### Stage 1: Daily Hygiene Non-Negotiables
- Morning routine: shower, brush teeth, deodorant, clean clothes
- Evening routine: brush teeth, face wash, set out tomorrow's clothes
- Deliverable: printed checklist on bathroom mirror

### Stage 2: Laundry System
- Set specific laundry days (not "when it piles up")
- Sort: darks, lights, delicates
- Full cycle same day: wash > dry > fold > put away
- Deliverable: laundry day on calendar, steps posted in laundry area

### Stage 3: Grooming and Presentation
- Haircut schedule (every X weeks, on calendar)
- Nail care, clothing rotation, matching basics
- Occasion-appropriate dressing
- When to retire worn items

### Stage 4: Fitness Foundations
- Establishing a consistent workout schedule
- Learning proper form (with Matteo or gym resources)
- Tracking workouts (simple log: what, how long, how it felt)
- Sleep hygiene: consistent bedtime, phone down 30 min before sleep

### Stage 5: Nutrition Basics
- Understanding balanced meals (protein, carbs, vegetables)
- Meal prep basics (cook once, eat multiple meals)
- Hydration tracking
- Understanding how food affects energy and mood

## Pillar 2: SYSTEMS (Orange)

### Stage 1: Kitchen Basics
- Dishes: wash or load dishwasher after every meal (not "later")
- Stove: wipe down after cooking (show how, what products)
- Counters: clear and wipe nightly
- Trash: out when 3/4 full, not overflowing
- Deliverable: "After Cooking" checklist posted in kitchen

### Stage 2: Kitchen Advanced
- Fridge organization and weekly clean-out
- Grocery list system (running list on fridge or phone)
- Meal planning basics
- Pantry organization

### Stage 3: Bathroom and Living Spaces
- Toilet, sink, mirror weekly clean
- Daily tidy: 10-minute reset before bed
- Clothes go in closet/hamper, not couch
- Making the bed daily

### Stage 4: Deep Cleaning (Monthly)
- Vacuum/mop schedule
- Dusting, bathroom deep clean, kitchen deep clean
- Seasonal tasks (windows, closet rotation)

### Stage 5: Time Management and Routines
- All commitments go on shared calendar
- Check calendar every morning
- If you commit to a time, show up or communicate BEFORE
- Weekly planning: Sunday morning review of week ahead
- The standard: do what you said you'd do, when you said you'd do it

### Stage 6: Adulting Essentials
- Grocery shopping (list, shop efficiently, store properly)
- Basic cooking (3-5 simple meals he can make independently)
- Managing appointments and scheduling
- Clean as you go (not after)
- Deliverable: recipe cards for starter meals

## Pillar 3: NETWORK (Yellow)

### Stage 1: Basic Communication
- If plans change, text BEFORE, not after
- Script: "Hey, something came up. Can we adjust tonight's plan?"
- The standard: communicate changes before the other person discovers them
- Practice exercise: role-play a plan change scenario

### Stage 2: Rapport Conversations
- What rapport is (connecting, not reporting)
- Ask one question about the other person before talking about yourself
- Listen for the answer, don't just wait to talk
- Mirror back what they said ("sounds like you had a great day")
- Practice: 5-minute rapport conversation, M gives feedback

### Stage 3: Deeper Friendship Skills
- Maintain friendships: initiate contact, don't just respond
- Make plans: propose specific time and activity (not "we should hang out")
- How to be a good guest/host
- Stages of friendship depth and what each level requires
- Practice: initiate one plan with a friend this week

### Stage 4: Community Engagement
- Joining groups or activities outside the relationship
- Showing up consistently (not just once)
- Being known for reliability in a community
- Volunteering or contributing to something bigger

## Pillar 4: CORE / SELF (Green)

### Stage 1: Emotional Awareness
- Recognizing the freeze response ("I notice I'm shutting down")
- Naming feelings: tired, overwhelmed, anxious, avoidant (not just "fine")
- Journaling basics: even 3 sentences about how the day went
- Practice: name one feeling per day out loud

### Stage 2: Repair After Conflict
- What repair looks like: name what happened, own your part, ask what they need
- Script: "I know [thing] happened. I'm sorry for [action]. What would help right now?"
- The standard: repair initiated within 24 hours, by the person who caused the rupture
- Practice: role-play a repair conversation

### Stage 3: Sitting in Discomfort
- Asking for a timed pause instead of fleeing ("I need 20 minutes, then I'll come back")
- Returning to the conversation without being prompted
- Not substituting comfort for accountability (no Taco Bell after a fight)
- Practice: M raises a medium-difficulty topic, Elvis stays present

### Stage 4: Self-Accountability
- Promise keeping: do THAT thing, not a substitute
- If you can't deliver, communicate BEFORE the deadline
- Track your own commitments (not relying on M to track for you)
- The standard: words and actions match at least 90% of the time

### Stage 5: Personal Growth
- Seeking professional help independently (therapist, coach)
- Reading or listening to growth material without being assigned it
- Identifying patterns in yourself without being told
- The standard: growth is internally motivated, not externally managed

## Pillar 5: DREAMS (Blue)

### Stage 1: Vision Basics
- What do you actually want your life to look like in 1 year? 3 years? 5 years?
- Write it down. Not in your head. On paper or in a doc.
- Deliverable: a simple vision statement (even 5 sentences)

### Stage 2: Goal Setting
- Turning vision into 3 concrete goals for the next 90 days
- Making goals specific and measurable (not "be better" but "apply to 5 jobs by March")
- Breaking each goal into weekly actions
- Deliverable: 90-day goal sheet, reviewed at Sunday sessions

### Stage 3: Planning and Execution
- Reverse engineering: start from the goal, work backward to today
- Weekly check-ins: am I on track? What needs to adjust?
- The difference between dreaming and planning (planning has steps and dates)
- Accountability: sharing goals with M and tracking progress

### Stage 4: Aspiration and Ambition
- What does a life you're proud of look like?
- What would you regret NOT doing?
- Identifying the gap between where you are and where you want to be
- Creating a personal development plan that's self-directed

## Pillar 6: VOCATION (Purple)

### Stage 1: Work Ethic Foundations
- Showing up on time, every time
- Doing what's asked, plus a bit more
- Communicating proactively with managers/team
- The standard: be the person people can count on

### Stage 2: Professional Development
- Identifying skills that need improvement
- Taking courses, reading, or practicing outside of work hours
- Asking for feedback and implementing it quickly
- Building a professional reputation for reliability

### Stage 3: Career Strategy
- Where do you want to be in 2 years professionally?
- What skills does that role require that you don't have yet?
- Networking within your industry (not just showing up to work)
- The Promoted Employee framework: stop being managed, start managing the situation

### Stage 4: Professional Relationships
- How to communicate with managers effectively
- How to receive feedback without defensiveness
- How to advocate for yourself (raises, promotions, projects)
- Building a reputation: what do people say about you when you're not in the room?

## Pillar 7: WEALTH (Pink)

### Stage 1: Basics (Financial Awareness)
- Needs vs. wants
- Track every dollar for 30 days
- Simple budget: income minus expenses
- Understanding bank accounts (checking vs. savings, reading statements)
- Auto-pay for recurring bills
- Envelope system for variable spending
- Understanding credit scores: what, why, how to check
- No new debt: if you can't pay cash, you can't afford it
- Deliverable: first budget created together, reviewed weekly for first month

### Stage 2: Middle (Financial Discipline)
- Dave Ramsey Baby Steps:
  - Step 1: $1,000 emergency fund
  - Step 2: Debt snowball (smallest balance first)
  - Step 3: Full emergency fund (3-6 months expenses)
- Understanding interest and how credit card debt compounds
- Debit card discipline: switch to debit for daily spending
- Meal planning as financial strategy
- Subscription audit
- Weekly money check-in with partner (no shame, just data)
- Understanding pay stubs (gross vs. net, taxes, deductions)
- Net worth tracker (assets minus liabilities)
- Deliverable: debt snowball worksheet, emergency fund account opened

### Stage 3: Advanced (Financial Strategy)
- Investing basics: 401k, employer match, Roth vs. Traditional IRA
- Compound interest working FOR you
- Index funds vs. individual stocks (keep it simple)
- Insurance basics: health, renters/homeowners, auto, life
- Tax basics: W-2 vs. 1099, standard deduction
- Understanding your benefits package at work
- Planning for large purchases: saving vs. financing
- Building toward homeownership
- Estate planning: will, beneficiaries, power of attorney
- Pabrai principle: asymmetric thinking, low downside, high upside decisions
- Generational wealth mindset: every decision affects the next generation
- Deliverable: investment account opened, insurance audit, beneficiaries updated

**Teaching approach for finances:** Start with zero shame. Use real numbers (his actual statements, his actual score). Make it visual. Celebrate every milestone.

## Sunday Session Structure (45 min)

**Minutes 1-5: Review**
"How did last week go? What felt easy? What was hard?"
Celebrate wins. Even small ones. Especially small ones.

**Minutes 5-10: This Week's Pillar and Focus**
State the SEEDS framework:
- "Here's where we are: [current state]"
- "Here's what we're working toward: [end goal]"
- "Here's how we get there: [steps]"

**Minutes 10-35: Teach and Practice**
Physical tasks: side by side, not watching from across the room.
Communication skills: role-play, practice scripts, gentle feedback.
Financial skills: real numbers, real accounts, real spreadsheets.

**Minutes 35-40: Set Commitments**
2-3 specific, measurable actions for the week. Not "be better at communication." Instead: "Text me if plans change before you leave."

**Minutes 40-45: Close**
"Any questions? Anything unclear? I'm proud of you for working on this."
Log commitments in Promise Audit.

**After session:** Send summary text with commitments. Post checklists in relevant locations. During week: observe without nagging. Log data. Review at next Sunday.

## Pillar Rotation Suggestion

Rotate focus pillars week by week to maintain progress across all seven:
- Week 1: Health + Systems (physical foundations)
- Week 2: Network + Core (emotional and social)
- Week 3: Vocation + Wealth (professional and financial)
- Week 4: Dreams + Review all pillars (vision and audit)

This ensures no pillar gets neglected and each gets attention at least monthly.

## Key Principles

**Patience over perfection.** He's learning skills many people learn in childhood. Slow progress is still progress.

**Systems over willpower.** Build checklists, routines, calendar reminders, physical cues. The system does the remembering.

**Celebrate the wins.** 3 out of 5 days is progress from zero. Name it. Then raise the bar.

**Don't do it for him.** The goal is competency, not compliance. Teaching, then stepping back.

**Track everything.** Promise Audit exists for a reason. Feelings rewrite memory. Data doesn't.

**This is teaching, not punishment.** Tone: "I believe you can learn this. I'm going to help you. And I'm going to hold you accountable because I care about your growth."

## Generating Lesson Plans

When asked for a specific lesson, generate:
1. **Pillar:** Which of the 7 pillars?
2. **Stage:** Which stage within that pillar?
3. **Current state:** Where is Elvis now?
4. **End goal:** What does competency look like?
5. **Steps:** Every step, sequentially, nothing assumed
6. **Demo plan:** Physical demonstration or written reference?
7. **Commitments:** 2-3 specific, measurable actions for the week
8. **Materials:** Checklists, supplies, scripts needed
9. **Tracking:** What gets logged in the Promise Audit?

## Generating Checklists

When asked for a checklist, create:
- Plain language (no jargon, no assumptions)
- Numbered sequential steps
- Supplies/products needed
- Frequency (daily, weekly, after every use)
- Printable for posting in relevant location
- Checkboxes for daily tracking if appropriate
