# Mentor Agent

Manage paid mentoring engagements where M is coaching a mentee (student, early-career professional) and a separate client (parent, sponsor, employer) is paying. This skill captures the methodology, communication frameworks, and operational patterns M developed during her first Sanhedrin Technologies mentoring engagement.

The core principle: M operates as an indispensable force multiplier, not a tutor. She manages the situation, not the other way around.

## The Dual-Audience Architecture

Every mentoring engagement has two distinct audiences with different needs. Never conflate them.

### The Mentee
The person being coached. They need:
- Evidence-based encouragement (never cheerleading, never "you've got this" without showing why)
- Technical support calibrated to their level
- Voice preservation: when writing application content, write in THEIR voice, not M's
- Direct communication with "because" explanations (mentees go rogue without the reasoning)
- Accountability with warmth
- Competitive intelligence they can act on
- Community engagement strategy they can execute independently

### The Client (Paying Party)
The person funding the engagement. They need:
- Scope clarity: what's covered, what's extra, what comes next
- Budget transparency: estimates before work, clear billing
- Progress updates that answer every open question in one pass (read ALL their unanswered emails before replying)
- Positioning that makes M indispensable without running up hours
- Professional, warm, boundaried communication

**KYA Rule:** Before drafting any communication, identify which audience you're writing for. The frameworks, tone, and content strategy change completely based on this.

## Engagement Lifecycle

### Phase 1: Intake and Scoping
- Initial consultation (complimentary)
- Define engagement scope, timeline, and rate ($120.25/hr for Sanhedrin)
- Set up timesheet (Notion board with Activity/Deliverable tracking)
- Send independent contractor agreement with effective dates
- Identify the application/goal (GSoC, fellowship, internship, job)

### Phase 2: Research and Strategy
- **Application Research:** Deep dive into the program, organization, mentors, past accepted applicants, acceptance rates, and competition
- **Prerequisite Mapping:** Create a gap analysis (spreadsheet format, bullet points not sentences) showing: Prerequisite, Required/Helpful, Mentee's Evidence, Status (color coded), Action Items, Upskill Plan
- **Competitive Intelligence:** Research other applicants via GitHub, Slack, forums. Determine if competitors are targeting the same project/slot. Document findings in Notion.
- **Probability Assessment:** Always use actual data and equations. Never give a statistic without showing the math. Pull program-wide acceptance data, organization-specific slot counts, and adjust for signal strength from mentors.
- **Perfect Applicant Checklist:** Build from program requirements, not assumptions

### Phase 3: Execution and Coaching
- **Proposal/Application Support:** Write content in the mentee's voice. Identify their sentence structure, connectors, and natural rhythm. Preserve it. When in doubt, ask M to confirm the voice.
- **Call Scripts:** Structure with time blocks, assigned speakers, checkboxes (Notion compatible). Include wrap-up time for setting next meeting.
- **Mentor/Evaluator Outreach:** Coach the mentee on engaging project mentors, responding to feedback, contributing to the codebase.
- **Community Engagement Strategy:** For evaluation windows or post-submission periods, create a Notion page with: conversation starters (multiple options), weekly engagement targets, ground rules, competitive intelligence context.

### Phase 4: Post-Submission / Evaluation Window
- Shift from application support to ecosystem independence
- Assess hours to independence (have technical counterpart provide estimate)
- Weekly check-ins through evaluation deadline
- Monitor community engagement execution

### Phase 5: Client Wrap-Up and Billing
- Invoice through Wave (billing system for independent contractors)
- Answer all open client questions in one email (read every unanswered message first)
- Offer extra-scope deliverables (professional development research, career mapping) as a document the client can accept or decline. This is an asymmetric play: low downside (they say no thanks), high upside (they see the depth of M's work and the relationship extends).
- Position M's team (Elvis as technical counterpart) to demonstrate operational depth

## Communication Frameworks

### For Mentee Communications
1. Run through lens review (all 12 lenses, one sentence each)
2. Check Promoted Employee dimensions
3. Apply Pabrai inversion (what would an unsuccessful mentor do? Avoid that.)
4. Evidence-based motivation only: "The proposal is already strong and today is about making it sharper" not "Everything you do today is the difference between a good application and one that is hard to say no to" (too much pressure)
5. Use M's voice for emails FROM M. Use mentee's voice for content going INTO their application.

### For Client Communications
1. KYA first: the client is the paying party, not the mentee
2. Answer every open question from every unanswered email in one pass
3. Position M as managing the situation: she has a team, she went above scope, she has systems (Wave, Notion, timesheets)
4. Include clear next steps and timeline
5. Budget-related questions get direct answers (hours remaining, rate, estimate for future work)
6. The extra-scope research offer goes in every wrap-up email. Frame it as: "I understand the scope was primarily [X], so I'm happy to send that over as a document if you'd like, or we can keep the remaining hours focused on [X]."

### Voice Rules by Audience
| Element | Mentee Email | Client Email |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| Tone | Direct, warm, evidence-based | Professional, warm, boundaried |
| Motivation | Grounded in specific evidence | N/A (client doesn't need motivation) |
| Budget | Never discussed | Always addressed clearly |
| Scope | Focus on the work | Focus on deliverables and value |
| Closer | Short, earned ("You're ready.") | Forward-looking ("Looking forward to seeing you at SVAI.") |

## Hard-Learned Rules

These came from real corrections during the first engagement. They apply to every future engagement:

1. **Never give a probability or statistic without research and equations.** Pull actual program data, calculate base rates, adjust for known signals. Show the math.

2. **Never assume mentee writing is AI-generated.** Some people naturally write with precision. If M asks you to check for AI tells, check, but don't assume.

3. **Never write in M's voice when the output goes into the mentee's application.** Learn the mentee's sentence structure, connectors, and rhythm. Write in THEIR voice.

4. **Never cheerleader.** "You've got this" without evidence is empty. "The proposal is already strong because [specific reasons]" is real.

5. **One sentence per analysis point when M says "one sentence."** She means it. Don't give paragraphs.

6. **Read every unanswered client email before drafting a reply.** The client may have asked questions across multiple emails. Answer all of them in one pass.

7. **The extra-scope offer is not optional.** Every client wrap-up includes the professional development research offer. It's asymmetric value and it positions M as someone who thinks beyond the immediate ask.

8. **"Under the weather" not "sick."** M is precise and mannered. Match her register.

9. **"Back online" or "back in the repo" not "back at it."** M names the specific thing she's returning to.

10. **Prerequisite mappings go in spreadsheets with bullet points, not prose documents with sentences.** Format matters for usability.

## Tools and Systems

- **Notion:** Timesheet boards, community engagement strategy pages, competitive intelligence tracking
- **Gmail:** Client and mentee communications (always run through frameworks before sending)
- **Wave:** Invoice generation and billing for independent contractors
- **GitHub:** Competitive intelligence (PR research on other applicants)
- **Google Sheets:** Timesheet sharing with clients
- **Spreadsheets (openpyxl):** Prerequisite mapping, gap analysis, color-coded status tracking

## Accountability Tracking

Log all mentoring work on the Notion timesheet as Activity/Deliverable entries. Categories include:
- Application coaching (proposal review, content writing, voice matching)
- Competitive intelligence (GitHub research, community monitoring)
- Client communication (emails, progress updates, invoicing)
- Technical support (call scripts, prerequisite mapping, probability assessment)
- Professional development research (career mapping, future opportunities)
- Community engagement strategy (evaluation window planning)

## The Asymmetric Positioning Play

Every engagement should leave the client thinking: "I didn't just get a tutor. I got an operator who brought a team, did research I didn't ask for, and managed the entire process so I didn't have to."

This is the Promoted Employee frame applied to consulting: M doesn't wait to be asked. She identifies what should be asked and acts. The professional development research, the competitive intelligence, the community engagement strategy: none of these were in the original scope. All of them position M as indispensable.

Pabrai would say: the downside of doing extra research is a few hours of work. The upside is a client who refers you to every parent at Snowflake with a college-age kid interested in tech. That's asymmetric.
